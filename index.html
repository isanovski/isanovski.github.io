Sample support replies

Topics drawn from public Streamflow documentation — vesting, claiming, token locks, cancel flow. Written as live chat replies.

“My claim button is greyed out — nothing to claim?”

Two things to check. First, is the vesting cliff over yet? Nothing unlocks before the cliff date, even if the contract is active. Second, open the contract detail page and look at Claimable — that number is what the button reads. If Claimable is 0, there is nothing to withdraw yet and the button is working correctly. If it shows a number but stays disabled, send me the contract address and I will check the on-chain state.

“I created a vesting contract for my team. Can I cancel it later?”

Only if cancellation was enabled when the contract was created. At setup the sender chooses whether the sender, the recipient, or neither may cancel — that permission is fixed at deployment and cannot be added afterwards. Open the contract and check the details panel. If neither party was given it, the contract runs to completion. If it was enabled, cancel from the three-dot menu on the contract row.

“I used my exchange deposit address as the vesting recipient. Now what?”

Centralised exchange wallets cannot sign a claim transaction, so a contract paying into an exchange address has to use Auto-Claim — then unlocked tokens are pushed straight to that address instead of waiting for a signature. If Auto-Claim was not enabled, the tokens are not lost: they stay in the contract, but only the recipient address can claim them, and an exchange will not do that for you. The practical fix is a new contract paying to a wallet you control, then cancel or let the old one run out.

“Why did I receive tokens I never bought?”

Almost always an airdrop. Before you touch them, check two things: whether the token's mint authority is still active, and whether the token appears in the project's own official announcement. Unsolicited tokens that ask you to visit a link and connect your wallet to “claim more” are the most common drain pattern in this space — the token is the bait, not the reward. If you want rid of it, burn the token account rather than interacting with any site it points to.

“The stream stopped paying out mid-schedule. Is the contract broken?”

Usually not broken — empty. A streaming contract pays from the balance funded into it; if the sender did not top it up, the stream pauses when the balance hits zero and resumes once more is added. Open the contract and compare Amount deposited with Amount streamed. If deposited is lower, the sender needs to top up. If deposited is higher and it still is not paying, that is worth escalating — send the contract address.

FAQ sample — “my claim is not showing up”

Written as a self-serve article: cause first, fix second, escalation last.

Why the number is zero

A vesting contract releases tokens on a schedule, and most schedules start with a cliff — a period where nothing unlocks at all. Before the cliff date the claimable amount is genuinely zero. This is the single most common reason someone believes their allocation disappeared.

Check in this order

  • Cliff date. Contract detail page → schedule. If today is before the cliff, wait — there is nothing to fix.
  • Claimable amount. If it shows a number but the button is disabled, the issue is the wallet connection, not the contract.
  • Right wallet. The contract pays one specific address. Connect a different wallet and you will see the contract but not the allocation.
  • Right contract. Airdrop allocations and vesting contracts are separate objects. Check both before concluding anything is missing.

When it is genuinely a problem

If the cliff has passed, the connected wallet is the recipient address, and Claimable is above zero but the claim transaction fails — that needs a human. Send the contract address and the transaction signature. Do not send your seed phrase; no support process requires it.

“locked” and “vested” are not the same thing and the difference is usually where people get hurt. locked = cannot move until a date. vested = unlocks gradually, on a schedule, whether you are watching or not.
